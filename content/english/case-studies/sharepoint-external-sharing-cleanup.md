Anyone links were on. A freelancer from two years ago could still open last year's campaign. A client asked who outside the agency could see their files. Nobody in the room could answer with a list.

That is the usual starting point for a **SharePoint external sharing** cleanup. A Los Angeles [media and advertising](/industries/media-advertising/) agency called Secure Techies after a brand questionnaire demanded evidence, not a shrug. They lived in SharePoint. They did not own the slider.

This case study records the inventory, the Anyone cut, the guest list, and the briefing producers actually attended. Agency identifiers stay out. The method does not.

## Why they called

Agencies live on files, freelancers, and Thursday deadlines. Microsoft is explicit that [external sharing in SharePoint and OneDrive](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/external-sharing-overview) is on by default. Default is not a policy. It is a hope.

The [FTC small-business cybersecurity](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/small-businesses/cybersecurity) guidance is plain: you still own the data you hold. Headcount of producers is not a control.

Two facts set the date:

1. A client security packet asked who could access their assets. The last answer would have been improvised.
2. A new campaign was starting in four weeks. Tightening after that launch would have been an incident.

We had no leftover sharing report. We treated every Anyone link as live until the admin center said otherwise. A screenshot of a happy tenant is not an inventory. The export is.

Producers already knew those client stills should not live on an anonymous public link. The habit did not. We wrote the non-goals so nobody would later read a slider change as a DAM project.

## What this engagement was, and was not

Secure Techies sells Microsoft 365 operations as part of [cybersecurity](/services/cybersecurity/) and [managed help desk](/services/managed-help-desk/). We do not sell a digital asset manager and we do not pretend a three-week slider change is records management.

We wrote that limit into the statement of work. The ECD wanted client files off anonymous links, and producers who could still deliver.

Out of scope on purpose:

- A full information architecture
- Replacing Frame.io, Box, or whatever the editors actually use
- Turning off all external sharing
- A same-week cut with no guest path

In scope:

- Site and Anyone-link inventory
- Guest list with last sign-in
- Tenant and site sliders
- A written sharing rule
- A producer briefing

Microsoft documents how to [limit accidental exposure when sharing outside the organization](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/solutions/share-limit-accidental-exposure?view=o365-worldwide), including turning off Anyone links. That page is the method. This job was using it.

## How we cleaned SharePoint external sharing

### Week 1: the sharing export

The first half-day was a list, not a slider. Sites. Owners. Anyone links. Guests. Which libraries held client work vs the junk drawer.

We used the SharePoint sharing reports and the Entra guest list, then walked campaign sites by hand. A report that says "sharing is on" is not an inventory. We needed the URL, the owner, whether the link was Anyone or a named guest, and whether it still opened. We opened a sample of Anyone links from a browser that was not signed in. If it rendered stills, it counted.

That list prevented the usual failure: turning sharing off globally and watching producers flee to personal Dropbox.

We asked the ECD, before we opened the admin center, who they thought could still see files. They named current freelancers. They did not name 2022 color-house guests.

### Weeks 2 and 3: sliders and guests

Tenant sharing moved off Anyone. Microsoft is explicit that a more restrictive slider stops new Anyone links, and that guests typically lose access within about an hour when you tighten sharing. Anyone links are the anonymous version of that. We treated old pasted URLs as dead after the change, and we said so in the briefing before we moved the slider, not after a freelancer called the ECD. New and existing guests stayed where a campaign still needed a freelancer. Finance and HR sites went to people in the organization only.

Default link type moved to people with existing access, or specific people, not Anyone. A producer who hits Share should not land on an anonymous URL because the tenant still thought that was helpful.

Microsoft lets you [limit sharing by domain](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/restricted-domains-sharing). We did not need a 5,000-domain allowlist. We needed named guests on named sites. Domain restriction stayed as a later option if they only ever work with two post houses.

Stale guests died. Remaining guests got an owner and a review date. Offboarding a freelancer now includes the Entra guest, not just Slack.

We did not cut every live guest on day one. Two editors were on the current campaign. Cutting them would have taught the floor to use personal drives. We named them, tied them to a site, and put an end date on the calendar the week after delivery.

CIS [Access Control Management](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/access-control-management) is the same idea in framework language: know who can still get in. A Slack display name is not that list.

Help desk got a one-line ticket type: guest invite, guest removal. If sharing is only a policy PDF, it dies on the first Thursday.

## What the export hid

We are not going to invent a "sharing risk dropped 47 percent" slide. The useful story is the pattern.

**Anyone links outlived the job.** Producers pasted them because invite-a-guest felt slow. The links still opened. That is the opposite of an end date.

**Guests outlived the human.** Entra does not read the freelance agreement. Last sign-in told the truth. Some guests had not signed in for a year and could still open a library.

**Finance sat on the same slider as campaigns.** Payroll folders do not need a freelancer. Campaigns do. One tenant default made both look like "we share."

**OneDrive was informal.** A producer’s personal-looking OneDrive held current client stills because "it was faster than the site." That account followed the tenant slider. Recycle bin would not have helped a forwarded Anyone link.

**Owners were a rumor.** Several campaign sites had no named owner after a producer left. Sharing settings had no one to ask.

**Teams files were a confusion.** Teams *files* live in SharePoint. If the library is wide open, the channel is wide open. Teams *chat* is a different store. We asked the ECD, in writing, whether lost chat would stop a delivery. They said no. Lost campaign stills would. That is why we inventoried Teams-connected libraries the same day we inventoried classic sites. The packet records that so nobody later claims "everything in Teams" was locked.

Microsoft also documents how to [turn external sharing on or off](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/turn-external-sharing-on-or-off) at organization and site level. The most restrictive value always wins. We used that on purpose for finance.

CISA's [small and medium business](https://www.cisa.gov/audiences/small-and-medium-businesses) pages keep repeating access control in owner language. An Anyone link is access you cannot name.

None of this required a new product. It required treating client files as named access.

## What we told producers

Tools narrow the funnel. The desk stops the paste.

The written rule we left is short enough to tape near a monitor:

1. Client work is invited guests, not Anyone links.
2. Finance and HR are internal only.
3. Every guest has an owner and an end date. The freelancer rolling off is a ticket, not a vibe.
4. If a share fails on a Thursday, call help desk. Do not paste a personal Dropbox link to "just get it out."

We ran a fifteen-minute briefing with the people who actually send files. We showed one Anyone link from the export, opened it signed out, then showed the same URL after Anyone was off. That is the demo that sticks. We did not do an hour of slides.

Help desk sat through the first campaign week after the slider. If a share failed, the ticket was "invite this guest to this site," not "turn Anyone back on until Friday." We wrote that refusal down so a night technician would not undo the job.

### What we refused to do

We would not turn off all external sharing to look decisive. That is how files leave through a personal Dropbox you cannot see.

We would not invent a "sharing score dropped 47 percent" graphic. The useful number is how many Anyone links still opened, and whether a guest from 2022 still could.

We would not skip finance. Campaign sites get the attention because they are loud. Payroll is quiet and worse when it is wrong.

We would not leave OneDrive as "personal, so out of scope." If it holds client stills, it is in scope.

## What the packet contained

1. **Sharing inventory.** Sites, Anyone links, guests, owners.
2. **Slider map.** Tenant vs finance vs campaign vs archive.
3. **Guest list.** Live, cut, review date.
4. **Producer one-pager.** How to invite, who to call.
5. **Questionnaire paragraph.** Facts, not "we take security seriously."

[Zero trust](/blog/zero-trust-security/) at this size is not a platform. It is "do not trust a link because it came from Slack."

## What we verified before we called it done

A slider with no guest list is still a story.

| Gate | What "done" meant here |
| --- | --- |
| Inventory | Campaign sites, finance/HR, junk libraries, Anyone links, guests |
| Anyone | Off at tenant. Not re-enabled as a convenience |
| Guests | Stale cut. Live guests named with a site and owner |
| Sliders | Finance internal. Campaigns invited guests |
| Briefing | Producers who send files actually attended |
| Next job | Freelancer offboarding includes the Entra guest |

What they had that they did not have on day one:

- A list of who outside the firm could still open files
- Anyone links off
- A sharing rule producers had heard once
- Questionnaire answers with evidence

What they still did not have, and should not claim:

- A DAM
- A records schedule for every archive
- A guarantee a producer never pastes a personal link

## Lessons we would repeat

**Export Anyone links before you lecture.** The ECD's story and the admin center are often different people.

**Do not nuke all external sharing.** You will teach the shop to use Dropbox.

**Kill guests when the job dies.** Offboarding is not only staff.

**Split finance from campaigns.** One slider is how payroll looks like a mood board.

**Brief the people who paste links.** A policy PDF will not.
