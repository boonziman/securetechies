The dock cameras were fine. Fifty yards of gravel past the last lens went dark after the last truck. That is where the trailers sat overnight.

That is the usual starting point for a [mobile surveillance trailer](/services/mobile-cctv-trailers/). A regional 3PL in the Inland Empire called Secure Techies because the far fence was a facilities project and the calendar was a security project. They run a [warehousing](/industries/warehousing-logistics/) site: WMS in the office, scanners on the dock, and a yard the building NVR was never aimed at.

This case study records the dusk walk, the placement, the alert path, and the night test. Client identifiers stay out. The method does not. We will not invent a theft-drop percentage.

## Why they called

A warehouse is two businesses sharing a slab. Headquarters has accounting and a WMS login. The yard has parked trailers, copper, and a fence that looks like an invitation after 9 p.m.

CISA's [physical security](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/physical-security) topic is written for a wider set of sites than this gravel lot. The idea still holds: see the perimeter you actually have, not the one the building cameras imply. [Critical infrastructure](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/critical-infrastructure-security-and-resilience) language is heavier than a 3PL needs. The operator still had a perimeter they could not see.

Building cameras covered the dock and the office door. They did not cover the far fence. Cabling a pole meant a trench, a power drop, and a landlord conversation that was not going to finish this month.

Two facts set the date:

1. Overnight trailers were staying on the gravel through the next peak.
2. A posted guard was not in the budget, and a trench was not on the lease calendar.

We had no leftover site plan that matched the fence. We walked it.

The lease made the trench conversation worse, not better. A permanent pole is an improvement the landlord has to bless. A hitch on gravel is equipment the operator can move when the lease says so. That is why the trailer won the meeting. We wrote the other sentence into the packet too: this is not a substitute for a pole if they later want a fixed view. Say hitch when it is a hitch.

## What this engagement was, and was not

Secure Techies sells [mobile CCTV trailers](/services/mobile-cctv-trailers/) as solar, rapidly deployable units with live monitoring. We do not sell a claimed crime-rate drop. We do not replace a police report with a marketing graph.

We wrote that limit into the statement of work. The operator wanted a view and a call, not a story about arrests.

Out of scope on purpose:

- Replacing the building NVR
- Staffing a guard booth
- A permanent pole and trench
- Any promised percentage reduction in theft

In scope:

- A dusk sight-line walk
- Placement of one solar trailer
- Cellular uplink and live monitoring
- A named after-hours phone tree
- A night test we would write down
- A leave-behind sketch of what the unit can and cannot see

OSHA's [warehousing](https://www.osha.gov/warehousing) page is about people and vehicles, not cameras. We still treated the dock as a workplace while we placed a trailer. We did not block a bay to look busy.

## How we placed the trailer

### The dusk walk

We stood at the far fence at dusk. We stood in the trailer row. We stood where a forklift swings. We marked what the building cameras already saw so we would not sell a second view of the same dock.

The dead zone was honest: gravel, fence, the backs of parked trailers. That is where a catalytic converter or a cut fence happens when nobody is looking.

We also asked who currently gets a call at 1 a.m. The answer was "the recording." That sentence is why live monitoring was in scope.

### Placement day

The unit is solar. It does not need a warehouse circuit. It does not need a trench. That is the product fact. Placement is still a job.

We waited for a quiet window so a trailer on a hitch was not in a pickup lane. Mast up. Solar clear of the building shadow at the hours the panel has to work, not only at noon. August sun is generous. We still marked a winter re-check on the calendar so a low sun and a taller stack of trailers would not quietly starve the battery. Cameras aimed at the fence and the trailer row, not at the neighbor's yard.

Uplink was cellular on purpose. The warehouse Wi-Fi already had enough opinions. The trailer should not die because someone reset a dock access point. We confirmed a signal at the parking spot before we called the unit placed. A pretty mast with one bar is a recording that stops at midnight.

DHS [If You See Something, Say Something](https://www.dhs.gov/see-something-say-something) is a public habit, not a camera spec. The useful version here is smaller: if the monitor sees a person on the gravel after hours, a named human gets a call. Not a shared inbox. Not "we'll check in the morning."

## The night test

A placement without a night walk is a brochure.

After dark we walked the fence on purpose. We confirmed the picture, the infrared, and that an alert actually reached the on-call. Two-way audio was tested once so the operator knew what a hail sounded like. They chose to keep hails for confirmed presence, not for every raccoon.

The first night always teaches you what the yard thinks is motion. Headlights on the street. Dust. A cat. We sat with the operator and tightened the zone so the far fence and the trailer row were the trip, not the whole county. A phone tree that rings for wildlife will get silenced by week two. That is worse than no trailer.

Retention was written down: how many days, who can export, and that the office PC is not the only copy. A stolen office computer should not be the only place last night lived.

We did not run a fake "intrusion drill" with a contractor in a hoodie for a photo. We walked the line ourselves and wrote what we saw.

### Aiming, neighbors, and the street

A mast that can see a fence can also see a sidewalk. We aimed at the operator's gravel and the trailer row, not at the street or a neighboring yard. That is manners and it is also how you avoid a complaint that kills the project.

If the trailer row moves next season, the aim moves. We wrote a re-aim as a phone call, not a new construction job. That is the point of a hitch.

Live monitoring is a service with a person on the other end. We walked through what they would say on a hail and what they would not. The warehouse named an on-call manager. A shared "info@" mailbox is not an on-call manager.

CISA's [small and medium business](https://www.cisa.gov/audiences/small-and-medium-businesses) pages and the FTC's [small-business cybersecurity](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/small-businesses/cybersecurity) material are mostly about networks and identity. A yard camera does not replace those. It also does not wait on them. The operator still needed MFA on the WMS. That was a different ticket.

## What the unit does not do

**It does not see the whole county.** One mast has a field of view. The dock still belongs to the building cameras.

**It does not replace a guard.** Eyes and a hail are not a person at the gate. If that is the decision you are making, read [remote video monitoring vs guards](/blog/remote-video-monitoring-vs-guards/). This project was the fence gap.

**It does not create a theft statistic.** We will not write "incidents dropped 62 percent." The operator can compare police reports later if they want. This page is the placement.

**It does not run the WMS.** Warehouse IT and yard cameras are neighbors. They are not the same ticket. Help desk still owns scanners. The trailer owns the gravel.

The public [mobile surveillance trailers guide](/blog/mobile-surveillance-trailers-guide/) and the [solar trailer](/blog/solar-security-camera-trailer/) write-up are the product background. This page is one yard.

## What the packet contained

1. **Coverage sketch.** What the trailer sees, what the building cameras still own, what remains a dead zone.
2. **Alert path.** Primary, backup, what "after hours" means, when to hail, when to call police.
3. **Night-test notes.** Time, who walked, what the picture showed.
4. **Retention and export.** Days kept, who can pull a clip, where the copy lives.
5. **Move rules.** If the trailer row shifts next season, who calls for a re-aim.

[Parking-lot camera](/blog/parking-lot-security-cameras/) work is a cousin. A warehouse yard is not a retail lot. Trucks and a fence are the geometry. We did not copy a shopping-center layout onto gravel.

## What we verified before we left the yard

A mast in a photo is not a placement.

| Gate | What "done" meant here |
| --- | --- |
| Sight lines | Far fence and trailer row covered. Dock left to building cameras |
| Power and uplink | Solar clear of the building shadow. Cellular, not dock Wi-Fi |
| Aim | Operator gravel only. Not the street, not the neighbor |
| Night test | Walk after dark. Picture, infrared, alert to the named on-call |
| Hail | Two-way audio tested once. Used for confirmed presence, not wildlife |
| Retention | Days kept, who can export, copy not only on the office PC |

What they had that they did not have on day one:

- Eyes on the gravel after the last truck
- A phone tree that was not "check the recording in the morning"
- A night-test note with a time and a name
- A re-aim rule when the trailer row moves

What they still did not have, and should not claim:

- A posted guard
- A theft-rate graph
- Coverage of the whole county from one mast

## Lessons we would repeat

**Walk at dusk.** A noon site walk lies about shadows and headlights.

**Do not promise the building NVR.** If the dead zone is fifty yards past the last lens, say so.

**Write the phone tree before the mast goes up.** Hardware without a call is a recording.

**Keep the dock clear.** A security trailer that blocks a pickup window is an operations incident.

**Refuse the fake percentage.** A client who needs a graph more than a view is buying a different product than the one we placed.

**Cellular on purpose.** The dock Wi-Fi already had enough opinions. A trailer that dies when someone resets an access point is not a perimeter.

**Write the re-aim.** Yards change. A hitch is wasted if nobody is allowed to move the unit when the trailer row shifts.

**Tune the zone or the phone goes off.** A trailer that cries wolf will be ignored. Fence and trailer row. Not headlights on the street.

**Mark a winter sun check.** A panel that works in August can sit in a building shadow in January. The battery does not send a polite reminder.
